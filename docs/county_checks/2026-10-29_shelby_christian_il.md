# County check: Shelby and Christian counties, IL (2026-10-29, run `2026-10-29-counties`)

The pass checked two Illinois counties under `docs/COUNTY_CHECK.md` with `/examine-county` on October 29, 2026. The county reports in `deliverables/county_reports/shelby_christian_il_renewables_2026-10-29.md` show two restriction instruments on record in each county, all resting on a compiled source (the NREL siting ordinance inventory or the Sabin Center report) and none verified against the instrument. Neither county is on `data/review/examination_worklist.csv`, which lists only counties not yet examined (a county is examined once it has a published record or a reviewed negative check); both have published records. One `county-examiner` drafted each county, and a `blind-reviewer` that had not drafted the row checked each drafted row. The `record-editor` wrote the rows, corrections and verdicts in `data/review/queue.csv`.

Illinois counties grant siting approval for commercial wind, solar and (since June 1, 2026) stand-alone storage under 55 ILCS 5/5-12020 and 5-12024. Both counties zone their unincorporated land, so their civil townships (24 in Shelby, 17 in Christian) do not zone (55 ILCS 5/5-12001 and 60 ILCS 1/105).

## Counties

Restrictions are counted by instrument (a local or state law that materially constrains wind, solar or battery storage: a moratorium, a ban, a setback, a height, noise or size limit, or a district where the use is prohibited; one instrument may cover several technologies and is counted once). "Confirmed" means a blind reviewer read the source and every claimed value held. "Held" means the row stays `awaiting_review`. A negative check is a reviewed record that someone searched the county's codes, minutes and news for a restriction and found none. Leads are documents or projects located in the pass but not drafted as rows.

### Shelby County (17173)

The examiner read the county's 109-page zoning ordinance (PDF created June 5, 2026), every County Board minutes file posted from January 2023 to September 10, 2026 (56 files, read by OCR), the Zoning Board of Appeals minutes of eight meetings from June 2023 to August 2024, and Moweaqua's council minutes from January 2023 to September 2026 (57 files). Both confirmed rows cite the county's posted zoning ordinance, read in full (`access` opened, `source_kind` instrument).

- **Section 16 (q_93d5a7953c).** Commercial solar facilities and stand-alone storage over 1,000 kW must be at least 50 feet from nonparticipating property lines and 150 feet from occupied community buildings and dwellings on nonparticipating property. Wind facilities must be 2.1 times blade tip height from nonparticipating residences and occupied community buildings and 1.1 times from nonparticipating property lines, with shadow flicker capped at 30 hours a year. All three are allowed only in agricultural or industrial districts. The first review found one error: the description called the October 12, 2023 vote a first adoption, and the minutes record it as an amendment. The examiner corrected the description and also removed its statement that the setbacks and shadow flicker limit equal the state maximums, since the statute text was not read in the session; a fresh review confirmed the corrected row. The amendment text attached to Resolution 2026-15 is not in the posted board record, so the reading that the posted Section 16 is the amended text rests on the PDF's date and the single combined section, which match the minutes.
- **Section 16 J (q_961a84dda5).** Resolution 2025-19, adopted on April 10, 2025 by voice vote (21 yes, 0 no) after the board tabled it on February 13, 2025, adds a moratorium on new solar and wind facilities that takes effect only if an Illinois appellate court holds P.A. 102-1123 or 55 ILCS 5/5-12020 invalid. Neither reviewer found such a holding in the documents read, which include Tate Road Solar 1, LLC v. County of Winnebago, 2026 IL App (4th) 250873. The row carries status `pending` (adopted, not in effect). The first review confirmed the row and asked that the description's last sentence, addressed to the reviewer, come out; the examiner removed it, and a fresh review confirmed the corrected row. `scripts/promote_reviewed.py` holds back only lifted or expired rows, so on promotion the row publishes as a pending restriction, as Crawford County's pending wind ban from the October 22, 2026 sweep did.

Standards the code also sets, which are not restrictions under section 3 of `docs/COUNTY_CHECK.md`: an Agricultural Impact Mitigation Agreement, NFPA 855 for storage, fencing, vegetative screening for solar, buried DC lines, decommissioning assurance, road use agreements, a siting fee cap and noise held to Pollution Control Board rules. Ordinance 23-07-O (October 12, 2023) requires siting approval and burial at least 5 feet deep for DC power lines in unincorporated areas. The County Board adopted a one-year data center moratorium on September 10, 2026; it does not reach wind, solar or storage.

### Christian County (17021)

The examiner could not open any of the county's own records: christiancountyil.gov returned a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403), and web.archive.org, which lists copies of the wind ordinance and the zoning code, was blocked by the session's egress policy. The published NREL wind row and the Sabin row on the September 2020 board vote remain not verified.

What was opened: Pana council minutes from January 2023 to August 2026 (86 sets), Kincaid board minutes from January 2023 to September 2026 with its posted ordinances, and an Illinois Commerce Commission filing in Docket 22-0499 (Grain Belt Express, Exhibit 1.6, November 1, 2022). That filing cites "Christian County WECS Ordinance, 2008 ZN 004 (last revised Dec. 15, 2020)", a date that differs from the September 2020 vote in the Sabin row; neither date has been checked against the ordinance. No moratorium appears in the Pana or Kincaid minutes read.

## What blocked the pass

- Code publishers. American Legal (Moweaqua, Taylorville) and Municode (Pana, and the Municode client lookup for Shelby County's places) returned Cloudflare challenges to curl and WebFetch.
- County site. Christian County's site returned a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403) to curl and WebFetch.
- Archives. web.archive.org returned HTTP 429, was blocked by the session's egress policy or reset the connection.
- Posted records. For ten of Shelby County's eleven places the examiner found no code or minutes online. Shelby County posts no Zoning Board of Appeals minutes after August 2024. In Christian County, Kincaid posts no zoning code, Taylorville's site returned HTTP 429 and then reset the connection (so its council and plan commission minutes were not reached), and Assumption's site returned a proxy error (502). Four other villages (Edinburg, Morrisonville, Mount Auburn, Stonington) were checked through one web search and a Zoneomics placeholder page with no code text, and five (Palmer, Owaneco, Bulpitt, Jeisyville, Tovey) through the placeholder page only, so whether each zones is not established.
- News. Taylorville Daily News article pages returned HTTP 404, 520, 522 or 525, or timed out.
- State docket. The Illinois Commerce Commission e-docket search needs a reCAPTCHA or a form POST. For Christian County it was searched only through a site-restricted web search; for Shelby County it was not searched.
