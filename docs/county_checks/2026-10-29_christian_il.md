# County check: Christian County, IL (2026-10-29, run `2026-10-29-counties-christian`)

The pass re-checked one Illinois county under `docs/COUNTY_CHECK.md` with `/examine-county` on October 29, 2026. An earlier pass the same day (`docs/county_checks/2026-10-29_shelby_christian_il.md`, run `2026-10-29-counties`) returned only leads for Christian County: christiancountyil.gov answers with a Cloudflare challenge, and that cloud session could not reach web.archive.org. This pass ran on a local machine that reached web.archive.org with plain requests, and read the county's own code and minutes through archived copies. One `county-examiner` drafted the county, a `blind-reviewer` that had not drafted the row checked each drafted row, and the `record-editor` wrote the rows, the correction and the verdicts in `data/review/queue.csv`.

Illinois counties grant siting approval for commercial wind, solar and (since June 1, 2026) stand-alone storage under 55 ILCS 5/5-12020 and 5-12024. Christian County zones its unincorporated land and any municipality without its own zoning code (Zoning Code Section 1-1-3), so its civil townships do not zone (55 ILCS 5/5-12001 and 60 ILCS 1/105).

## Result

Restrictions are counted by instrument (a local or state law that materially constrains wind, solar or battery storage: a moratorium, a ban, a setback, a height, noise or size limit, or a district where the use is prohibited; one instrument may cover several technologies and is counted once). "Confirmed" means a blind reviewer read the source and every claimed value held. "Held" means the row stays `awaiting_review`. A negative check is a reviewed record that someone searched the county's codes, minutes and news for a restriction and found none. Leads are documents or projects located in the pass but not drafted as rows.

## Rows

The three county rows cite the zoning code "Amended and Revised September 18, 2024", read through a Wayback copy archived October 29, 2025 (https://web.archive.org/web/20251029110714/https://www.christiancountyil.gov/wp-content/uploads/ZONING-CODE-3.21.24-2-5-9-4-2.pdf; `access` archived, `source_kind` instrument). The examiner compared it with the March 21, 2024 copy and reported that Appendix A (wind) and Appendix D (storage) are the same and that Appendix B (solar) differs only in its fence, screening, equipment (the battery clause, Equipment (c), is gone), hearing and penalty text. The two Assumption rows cite the city's posted code, current through Ordinance 838 (August 7, 2025) (https://www.cityofassumption.org/documents/397/Assumption_Complete_Code_S-8.pdf; `access` opened, `source_kind` official_copy).

- **County wind, Appendix A, Ordinance O2008 ZN 004 (q_c1dbceef6f), confirmed.** Adopted May 20, 2008. The appendix header reads "last revised on December 15, 2020", but the 500 foot height cap and the court fallback clauses are absent from the December 15, 2020 code copy and present in the March 21, 2024 copy. Towers must be at least the greater of 5 times tower height or 3,250 feet from any Primary Structure (the owner may waive this, but never below 1.10 times tower height), 2,000 feet from residentially zoned districts, and the greater of 1,640 feet or 3 times tower height from adjacent property lines (the adjacent owner may waive this). Height is capped at 500 feet unless the FAA issues a No Hazard determination, and no shadow flicker may fall on a Primary Structure unless its owner waives the rule. If a setback or flicker rule is held invalid in court, the code substitutes 2.1 times blade tip height from nonparticipating residences and occupied community buildings, 1.1 times from nonparticipating property lines, and 30 hours a year of flicker.
- **County solar, Appendix B, Ordinance O2017 ZN 012 (q_6e1325849b), confirmed after a correction.** Commercial solar facilities must be 150 feet from any residence, church or other dwelling and 50 feet from nonparticipating property lines, and are allowed only in the AG-1 Agriculture or I-2 Industrial districts by special use. The first review returned `unverifiable`: the codified text prints no passage date, so the adoption date could not be checked, and the card cited no source for two statements in the description (that the 2017 text set 100 feet, and that the Equipment clause removed in 2024 concerned batteries). The examiner then cited the signed 2017 scan (archived https://web.archive.org/web/20220926145544/https://www.christiancountyil.gov/wp-content/uploads/solar-ord.-O2017ZN012.pdf, "Passed this 21st day of November, 2017") and the March 21, 2024 code copy, changing only the description. A different blind reviewer then read the scan's page images, both code copies and the June 18 and September 17, 2024 minutes, and confirmed the corrected row.
- **County battery storage, Appendix D (q_de514fb058), confirmed.** Tier 2 systems (over 600 kWh) must keep every component at least 200 feet from a property line or right-of-way and 500 feet from the outer wall of an occupied structure off the site (200 feet with a recorded waiver), with one-hour average noise no more than 60 dBA at the outside wall of any non-participating occupied building. The code page prints "2003 ZB 039" and a September 11, 2001 signature date. The November 7, 2023 special County Board minutes record Ordinance O2023 ZB 039 on battery storage passing with all ayes, and the March 20, 2024 minutes record an amendment to Appendix D, Section 4 (O2024 ZB 003, 15 to 0).
- **Assumption wind ban, City Code 10-10-1, Ordinance 745 (q_41bf4823dd), confirmed.** Adopted October 2, 2019. The city "will not permit the existence of wind farms and electric-generating wind devices" within its zoning jurisdiction and a 1.5-mile radius around it.
- **Assumption commercial solar ban, City Code 10-11-1, Ordinance 812 (q_48485c1955), confirmed.** Adopted April 4, 2024. Commercial solar installations are not permitted within the city's zoning jurisdiction and a 0.5-mile radius around it; personal installations are excepted.

Standards the county code also sets, which are not restrictions under section 3 of `docs/COUNTY_CHECK.md`: wind noise held to Illinois Pollution Control Board rules, aircraft detection lighting, road use agreements, decommissioning assurance, application and permit fees, and public notice; for solar, an 8-foot fence, an evergreen buffer, buried lines, an Agricultural Impact Mitigation Agreement and decommissioning security; for storage, screening, NFPA 855, a fence height limit and an emergency plan. Tier 1 storage (600 kWh or less) and personal wind and solar systems fall under accessory rules and are not counted. The Tier 2 storage setbacks are larger than the setbacks 55 ILCS 5/5-12024 sets for stand-alone storage from June 1, 2026 (150 feet from occupied community buildings and nonparticipating homes, 50 feet from their property lines, per row `IL-state_setback_standard-2` in `data/review/state_policies_candidates.csv`); the archived records end before that date, so whether the county has changed them is not known.

## The published Christian County rows

Two published rows rest on compiled sources (the NREL siting ordinance inventory and the Sabin Center report), not on the instrument.

- `nrel:wind:17021` (height limit 450 feet; 1.1 times tip height from non-participating property lines and structures): the height and property line values match the superseded 2008 scan (http://web.archive.org/web/20240515062208/https://www.christiancountyil.gov/wp-content/uploads/Wind-Farm-Ord.pdf, the file NREL cites), which reads "maximum height not to exceed 450 feet" and "1.10 times the WECS Tower Height from adjacent property lines". The structure value does not: the 2008 text sets 1,000 feet from Primary Structures, with 1.10 times tower height as the closest a tower may stand to one. The current code contradicts each value (500 feet; the greater of 1,640 feet or 3 times height; the greater of 3,250 feet or 5 times height).
- `sabin:REC-0076` (the September 2020 County Board vote, 10 to 6): the September 15, 2020 minutes record a motion "to adopt all of the WECS text amendments as presented by the ZBA", carried with 10 ayes and 6 nays. A further amendment (TX20-10) passed 14 to 0 on December 15, 2020.

These readings are in the examiner's packet only; none was drafted as a `restriction_sources.csv` row. Recording them there through `/verify-evidence` (an `instrument-reader` and a blind second reading for each) is the next step for both published rows. When the confirmed wind row q_c1dbceef6f is promoted, the build matches it with `nrel:wind:17021` by jurisdiction and technology (`scripts/nrel_ordinances.py`): a pair with the same restriction type and severity (a score from one to four: one for procedural friction, two for a material burden, three for a likely de facto ban, four for an explicit moratorium or ban; the rule that set each score is printed beside it) is published once, and any other pair is kept with both rows and listed on `data/review/nrel_restriction_conflicts.csv`. Check that worklist after the build.

## What blocked the pass

- County site. christiancountyil.gov returned a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403) to plain requests, so every county record was read through a Wayback copy.
- Archives. The Wayback copies of the 2021, 2022 and 2023 compiled County Board minutes, and of three intermediate code copies, were cut off at 1,048,576 bytes and could not be read. Requests to web.archive.org over port 80 were refused on and off, and the CDX index answered "Temporarily Offline" (503) and once 504. Two archived county PDFs (Article XI of the zoning code and a one-page October Zoning Board of Appeals file) are images only and were not read by OCR. The archived County Board minutes end on July 15, 2025, and the Zoning Board of Appeals minutes on January 31, 2025.
- Code publishers. American Legal (Taylorville) and Municode (Pana) returned Cloudflare challenges (HTTP 403), and the archived Taylorville Title 10 page has no section text.
- Posted records. Pana's old file links return 404 after a site move, and none of the 184 archived Pana PDFs read holds the text of Ordinance 1946 or 1970. Stonington's site loads its document lists by script. Kincaid posts no zoning code (found in the earlier pass), and no code or minutes were found for Edinburg, Morrisonville, Mount Auburn, Palmer, Owaneco, Bulpitt, Jeisyville or Tovey, so whether each zones is not established. Assumption's council minutes were not opened.
- State docket. The Illinois Commerce Commission docket was searched only through a web search. Both moratorium trackers found nothing for the county; the packet records no result for the news searches.
